01 · Verdict in 60 seconds

8.2/ 10 overall

Start it. This is the right business for you — you already have the skill, it needs no office, no inventory, no licence, no approval, and the buyer pays in advance. It is the lowest-dependency high-margin business on your entire list.

But delete the phrase "no competition" from this one. VAPT in India is crowded and price-pressured at the bottom: entry price for a basic web app is already ₹25,000–30,000 and there are hundreds of shops. You do not win by being another VAPT vendor. You win on the three things nobody serves well at the SMB end: speed, evidence quality, and the new attack surfaces (LLM/AI, cloud config, supply chain).

8.2/10Audit agency, services-first
9.0/10Automation as YOUR internal tool
5.5/10Automation sold AS the product

The 6 decisions that matter

  1. Services first, product later. Build the platform to serve YOUR delivery, never as the thing you sell. Productise it only after ~10 paid engagements.
  2. Do not chase banks, NBFCs, brokers, insurers or government. Legally closed without CERT-In empanelment (section 04).
  3. Own two wedges: (a) startups/SaaS/D2C stuck on enterprise security questionnaires, (b) LLM/AI red teaming, where no price is set yet.
  4. Drop hardware testing on day one. 4.5/10 for a solo Indian shop. Year 2 or never (section 06).
  5. Automate recon, scanning, evidence and reporting. Never automate the hunt for business-logic flaws — that is the part clients actually pay for.
  6. Land one foreign client by month 6. Same work, 3–5× the price, no Indian price war.

Reality check, cold

  • Year 1 realistic: ₹8–25 lakh total, not per month. Months 1–3 will be near zero.
  • ₹2–3 lakh/month arrives around month 7–12 with 4–6 recurring clients, not 40.
  • Gross margin 85–92% once tooling is built. Very few businesses beat that.
  • Your killer is not competition — it is being unknown. Months 1–3 buy proof, not code.
  • The market is shifting from annual checkbox tests to continuous programmes. Sell retainers, not one-shot PDFs.

04 · The CERT-In wall — read this before anything else

CERT-In (under MeitY) empanels information security auditing organisations. It is the one real gate in Indian security services. Know exactly where it binds and where it doesn't.

Empanelment REQUIRED — closed to you

  • Banks (RBI Cybersecurity Framework)
  • NBFCs, payment aggregators, payment gateways
  • SEBI-regulated entities — CSCRF audits must use empanelled orgs
  • Insurers (IRDAI)
  • Government departments, PSUs
  • Anything hosted on NIC infrastructure
  • UIDAI-linked systems
  • Critical infra — power, telecom, transport

Empanelment NOT required — wide open

  • SaaS companies, product startups, D2C brands
  • Ecommerce, marketplaces, edtech, healthtech (non-regulated parts)
  • Any company doing ISO 27001 or SOC 2
  • Any company answering an enterprise security questionnaire
  • App publishers, agencies, dev shops
  • Manufacturing, logistics, real estate, hospitality IT
  • Cyber-insurance-driven SMB audits
  • Every foreign client, without exception

Empanelment demands proven capability across network, infrastructure, web app and wireless security, staff holding CISSP / CISA / CISM, organisational maturity and a technical evaluation. It is an organisation-level status, not an individual one. A solo operator does not get it in year one, and chasing it early is the classic way a new shop burns 12 months and a lot of cash.

Rule: never claim or imply empanelment you don't have. It is checkable on the CERT-In site in 30 seconds, it is fraud, and it ends you in a small market. When a prospect demands it, say plainly: "We're not empanelled — for your SEBI audit you need one. We do the pre-audit remediation testing so that audit passes first time." That reframe converts the rejection into a sale often enough to be worth memorising.

Path to empanelment (year 2–3, if ever): incorporate Pvt Ltd → 2–3 certified staff (CISSP/CISA/CISM) → ISO 27001 for your own firm → documented audit methodology + sample reports → apply in an open cycle → technical evaluation. Treat it as a year-3 unlock for a ₹2 crore+ market, not a startup requirement.

The honest risk nobody in the brochures says out loud: at the bottom of this market, an "annual compliance-checkbox pentest" is being commoditised from both sides — cheap freelancers below, automated PTaaS platforms above. If your product is a PDF produced by running a scanner, you will be competing at ₹25,000 forever and AI tooling will halve that. The escape is not working harder — it is selling validated exploitation, business-logic findings, and continuous coverage, which no scanner produces.

Where the soft spots genuinely are

  • Speed. Most Indian shops quote 2–4 weeks. A 72-hour turnaround for a scoped web app is a real, defensible wedge for a solo operator with automation.
  • Price transparency. Nearly every Indian competitor hides pricing behind "contact us". Publishing fixed prices converts buyers who hate sales calls.
  • Retest included. Most charge again for the retest. Include one free retest within 30 days and say so on the homepage.
  • LLM/AI. Genuinely open in the Indian mid-market right now. This is a 12–18 month window, not forever.
  • Vertical depth. "We test healthtech apps" beats "we test apps." Pick one vertical after your first 5 clients.

06 · Every service line scored

Same rubric for all: demand today, competition, automation ratio, margin, how hard to sell as an unknown, capital needed.

Web app + API VAPT 9.0 / 10

The problem: a startup is asked for a pentest report by an enterprise buyer, an insurer, an ISO auditor, or a DPDP-conscious board — and has nothing.

Product: OWASP-based grey-box test, authenticated, 3–6 days, PDF + client portal, one free retest in 30 days, compliance mapping (ISO A.8.8 / SOC 2 CC7.1 / PCI 11.4 / DPDP).

Demand: HIGHCompetition: HIGHAutomation: 55–65%Margin: 88%Capital: ₹0

Verdict: your bread and butter. It pays the bills, it is what people search for, and it is the door through which every other service is sold. Accept the competition and win on turnaround + retest + honest fixed pricing.

Cloud configuration & posture review 8.5 / 10

The problem: nearly every small AWS/Azure account has public S3 buckets, over-permissive IAM, unrotated keys, no logging, open security groups. It is the highest-density source of real findings in the shortest time.

Product: read-only IAM role → Prowler (600 checks / 84 AWS services / 44 frameworks) + manual review → prioritised report with exact fix commands. 1–3 days, ₹40k–1L.

Demand: HIGHCompetition: MED-LOWAutomation: 80%Margin: 93%Capital: ₹0

Verdict: best ₹-per-hour in the whole business, lowest legal risk (read-only, no exploitation), easiest to deliver remotely. Make this your second offer, and often your first sale — clients approve it faster because nothing can break.

Compliance-attached testing (ISO 27001 / SOC 2 / DPDP) 8.8 / 10

The problem: the compliance consultant sells the certificate but cannot do the technical test. The auditor demands one. Somebody has to fill that gap on a deadline.

Product: VAPT + evidence pack formatted for the auditor, plus remediation verification. ₹60k–1.5L, repeats every year automatically.

Demand: HIGHCompetition: MEDAutomation: 50%Margin: 85%Recurring: YES

Verdict: the single best channel in the business. You don't find these clients — the compliance consultant hands them to you, forever. See section 14, channel #1.

LLM / AI red teaming 8.0 / 10

The problem: every Indian company shipped an AI chatbot or agent in 2025–26. Almost none has tested it. Prompt injection is #1 on the OWASP LLM Top 10 for the second year running, and an agent with tool access is a remote code execution surface wearing a friendly face.

Product: prompt injection (direct + indirect), jailbreak matrix, system-prompt extraction, RAG poisoning, tool/function abuse, excessive agency, insecure output handling (XSS via model output), PII leakage, token-flood cost DoS, guardrail bypass. Mapped to OWASP LLM Top 10. ₹75k–2.5L.

Tools: garak (~100 probes, NVIDIA), PyRIT (Microsoft — multi-turn Crescendo and TAP attacks), Promptfoo (YAML-driven, acquired by OpenAI March 2026 for ~$86M and kept MIT-licensed), DeepTeam (40+ vulnerability types mapped to OWASP LLM).

Demand: RISING FASTCompetition: LOW in IndiaAutomation: 70%Margin: 90%Buyer pool: still smallish

Verdict: highest-upside line. It is not yet a big enough market to live on alone in 2026 — but it is the line that gets you written about, invited to speak, and quoted at prices nobody can compare. Attach it to every web app engagement as a ₹40k add-on.

Mobile app VAPT 8.3 / 10

The problem: hardcoded API keys, unpinned TLS, insecure local storage, exported components, and Play Store data-safety declarations that don't match reality.

Product: MobSF static + dynamic, Frida/objection runtime, API layer, OWASP MASVS mapping. 3–5 days, ₹40k–1L.

Demand: HIGHCompetition: MED-HIGHAutomation: 70%Margin: 88%Needs: rooted test device (~₹12k)

Verdict: strong second line. MobSF does so much of the static work that your effective hourly rate is high.

Continuous testing retainer / vCISO 8.6 / 10

Product: monthly rescans, new-asset discovery, alerting, quarterly deep test, security questionnaire support, policy help. ₹25–60k/mo (testing) or ₹75k–2L/mo (vCISO).

Recurring: YESCompetition: MEDAutomation: 85%Margin: 92%

Verdict: the whole point of the automation you're building. Four retainers at ₹50k = ₹2L/month with ~12 days of work. This is how you hit your target without chasing new logos every month.

Network / internal infrastructure testing 7.0 / 10

Demand: MEDCompetition: HIGHAutomation: 60%Margin: 80%Often needs on-site

Verdict: commoditised, sometimes requires travel or a shipped jump-box, and the buyers most likely to want it are the regulated ones you can't serve. Offer it as a bundle item, don't lead with it.

Hardware / IoT / firmware testing 4.5 / 10

Why it scores low despite low competition — and this is the important one:

  • Tiny buyer pool. India's hardware product companies that budget for security testing number in the low hundreds. Most Indian "hardware" firms import reference designs and change the casing.
  • Capital + lab. ChipWhisperer $250–$3,800, Attify IoT kit ~$1,555, logic analyser, bench PSU, soldering/rework, JTAGulator, Bus Pirate, adapters. ₹1.5–4 lakh before your first rupee — and it needs physical space, which you said you don't want.
  • Not automatable. You asked for "provide details and it's automatic." Hardware is the opposite: physical access, desoldering, probing, glitching, per-chip datasheets. It is the most manual work in security.
  • Devices must be shipped to you and often destroyed. Logistics, insurance, NDAs on unreleased hardware.
  • The compliance money is closed. TEC/MTCTE routes through ILAC-accredited labs and IC3S, not consultants. Becoming a lab is a crore-scale, multi-year accreditation business.
  • Long sales cycles tied to hardware release schedules, not quarters.
Recommendation: do not offer hardware testing in year 1. If you want the halo, offer "IoT ecosystem testing" instead — the mobile app, the cloud API and the firmware update mechanism. That is 80% of real IoT risk, needs zero lab, and you can do it today from a laptop.

General QA / functional testing ("we can also act as tester") 4.0 / 10

Different buyer, different budget line, brutal competition, ₹300–800/hour offshore rates, and it destroys your positioning. A security specialist who also does manual QA reads as a body shop, and body shops get squeezed. Say no to this. The one exception: automated regression on the security controls you built for a retainer client — that is security work wearing a QA hat, and it's fine.

07 · Can it all be automated? The real answer

Your vision was: "provide the link, creds and needed things — and all work done automatically." Here is the unvarnished 2026 position.

Automate 100% — do this today

  • Subdomain + asset discovery, DNS, cert transparency
  • Port and service enumeration
  • Known-CVE matching on discovered versions
  • Template-based vulnerability checks (Nuclei)
  • TLS/SSL, headers, cookie flags, CORS misconfig
  • Secret scanning in JS bundles and repos
  • Dependency / SBOM / container CVEs
  • Cloud config checks against benchmarks
  • Mobile static analysis
  • LLM probe suites (garak/promptfoo/DeepTeam)
  • Evidence capture (request/response pairs, screenshots)
  • Report generation, dedupe, CVSS scoring, retest diffing

Cannot automate — this is what they pay for

  • Business logic flaws — price manipulation, coupon stacking, workflow bypass, negative quantity, race conditions in payments
  • IDOR / broken access control across multi-role apps (still the #1 real-world finding)
  • Chaining — the good testers turn 3–5 low findings into one critical path; no scanner reasons across steps
  • Multi-step authenticated flows, OTP/2FA logic, session edge cases
  • Tenant isolation in multi-tenant SaaS
  • Judging business impact — the difference between a ₹25k report and a ₹75k one
  • Validating and killing false positives
  • Sitting on a call and explaining it to a CTO
The 2026 industry consensus, quoted plainly: autonomous agents own breadth and continuous coverage; humans own validation, judgment and regulatory sign-off. Traditional DAST reports 40–70% false positives; purpose-built agentic systems that only report what they can exploit with reproducible proof get under 2% — but they still miss the business-logic class entirely. AI pentesting agents are not a replacement for human testers, and the whole category converged on that in 2026.
So the correct model — and this is the single most valuable line in this document:
Automation is your margin, not your product.
The client buys a validated, exploit-proven, business-context report. Automation is how you deliver it in 3 days instead of 12 and keep 88% margin. You never sell "our scanner ran" — because a scanner costs $95/month and they can buy it themselves.

What "automatic" realistically looks like in your delivery

DAY 0 intake form client fills scope, uploads authorisation, drops creds in vault scope guard system refuses any target not on the signed scope list DAY 0 auto recon subfinder, httpx, katana, naabu, tlsx ~30 min unattended DAY 0 auto scan nuclei + ZAP + semgrep + trivy + prowler ~2-4 hrs unattended DAY 1 auto triage dedupe, CVSS, false-positive filter, LLM pass DAY 1-4 YOU auth flows, IDOR, logic, chaining, exploitation <-- the actual product DAY 4 auto report findings DB -> branded PDF + portal, compliance mapping DAY 5 YOU 30-min walkthrough call, remediation Q&A +30d auto retest rerun scoped checks, diff, issue retest letter

Result: ~60% of the calendar time is unattended machine work. You touch the engagement for 3–4 days and bill ₹50–75k. Three of these a month plus one retainer is your ₹2–3 lakh.

08 · Platform architecture + every feature worth building

Before the feature list, the discipline: do NOT build this platform first. Building a platform before you have paying clients is the most common way technical founders spend nine months and earn zero. Deliver your first 10 engagements with shell scripts, a findings spreadsheet and a Word template. The platform earns its existence only when repetition hurts.

Build order (each stage only after the pain is real)

0
Engagements 1–3 — nothing. Bash script that runs your tool chain, a folder per client, a hand-written report from a template. Cost: ₹0. Time: one evening.
1
Engagements 4–10 — a findings database. Install DefectDojo (open source, ingests output from nearly every scanner, dedupes, tracks status). Do not write your own. This alone removes most of the pain.
2
Engagement 10+ — report generation. Findings DB → templated PDF. This is the highest-value automation you will ever write, because report writing is 30–40% of engagement hours.
3
Retainer clients exist — scheduling + diffing. Cron the scans, diff against last run, alert on new findings. Now you can sell continuous testing without doing continuous work.
4
5+ retainers — client portal. Login, findings list, status, retest button. This is when the platform starts to look like a product.

Feature list — everything worth having, grouped

Intake & safety

  • Scope form: domains, IPs, app URLs, exclusions, test window
  • E-signed authorisation capture, stored with the engagement
  • Scope guard — hard blocklist that refuses to run any tool against an unlisted target (your #1 legal safety net)
  • Credential vault with per-engagement expiry
  • Client contact + escalation path for out-of-band critical findings
  • Auto-generated pre-test checklist (backups taken? WAF whitelisted? staging or prod?)

Discovery & scanning

  • Passive recon: subdomains, cert transparency, DNS, WHOIS, exposed buckets
  • Active: port/service scan, tech fingerprint, crawl + JS endpoint extraction
  • Template scanning (Nuclei) + authenticated DAST (ZAP)
  • SAST + secrets + dependency + IaC (Semgrep, gitleaks, Trivy)
  • Cloud benchmark (Prowler), Kubernetes (kube-bench)
  • Mobile static + dynamic (MobSF, Frida)
  • Rate limiting and throttle controls per target (don't take a client down)

Triage & validation

  • Cross-tool dedupe and normalisation
  • CVSS 4.0 scoring + business-impact override field (the human bit)
  • False-positive marking that persists across future scans of the same asset
  • Exploit-proof attachment: request, response, screenshot, repro steps
  • Severity justification text — auditors ask for this

Reporting

  • Branded PDF + HTML, executive summary + technical detail
  • Compliance mapping: ISO 27001 A.8.8, SOC 2 CC7.1, PCI DSS 11.4, DPDP reasonable safeguards
  • Remediation with actual code/config snippets, not "sanitise input"
  • Retest report showing fixed / open / accepted with dates
  • Attestation letter — the one-page PDF the client forwards to their enterprise buyer. Clients value this more than the 60-page report.
  • Questionnaire helper: pre-written answers to common vendor security questionnaires

Continuous (retainer engine)

  • Scheduled rescans, diff vs previous run
  • New subdomain / new port / new service alerts
  • Certificate + domain expiry watch
  • Leaked-credential and exposed-secret monitoring
  • Monthly one-page posture summary, auto-emailed
  • SLA timers on open critical findings

LLM / AI module

  • Probe suites: garak breadth sweep + PyRIT multi-turn campaigns
  • Prompt-injection corpus, direct and indirect (poisoned docs/webpages)
  • Jailbreak matrix, system-prompt extraction, guardrail bypass
  • RAG poisoning, tool/function abuse, excessive agency tests
  • Insecure output handling (XSS/SSRF via model output)
  • PII leakage + training-data extraction probes
  • Token-flood cost/DoS test with billing impact estimate
  • OWASP LLM Top 10 coverage matrix in the report
Differentiator features worth more than any scanner: (1) 72-hour turnaround guarantee on scoped web apps; (2) one free retest inside 30 days, in writing; (3) the one-page attestation letter; (4) compliance-mapped findings; (5) a fix-verification call with the client's developers, not just a PDF drop. Every one of these is a sales feature, and none requires a line of scanner code.

10 · Do you really need Burp? Is Strix enough?

Burp Suite Professional — $449–475/year (~₹40,000)

Short answer: not to start. Yes by client #3–5.

You can genuinely skip it because

  • Caido has a free Community tier and a cleaner, faster workflow than Burp for manual testing
  • ZAP is the most complete free scanner — full active scan, scripting, automation framework
  • Nuclei covers templated checks better than Burp's scanner for known issues
  • Burp Community's Intruder is deliberately rate-throttled — but ffuf is faster than Burp Intruder anyway

You will eventually want it because

  • The BApp extension ecosystem (Autorize for IDOR/authz testing alone can justify the licence)
  • DOM Invader for client-side work
  • Turbo Intruder for race conditions — a real revenue-generating finding class
  • Clients and partners ask what tools you use. "Burp Pro" is a trust signal in an industry that runs on signals
  • The Burp certification (BSCP) is a cheap credibility artifact
Absolute rule: never use a cracked Burp. It is a licence-audit risk, it destroys you if a client ever sees it, and it is indefensible in a market whose entire product is trustworthiness. Free tools until you can pay, then pay.

Strix and AI agents — what they're actually good for

Strix is a real open-source agentic pentesting tool with a large community, running agents in parallel across targets, validating findings with proofs-of-concept across OWASP Top 10 classes. Use it. But understand its place:

  • Good for: a fast breadth pass on a new target, generating candidate findings, and catching the obvious things before you start manual work. It compresses day 1.
  • Not good for: being the deliverable. Everything it reports must be human-validated, and the classes it misses (multi-role business logic, tenant isolation, chained exploits) are exactly the classes your fee is justified by.
  • Costs: it is free software with a non-free bill — agentic runs burn LLM tokens. Budget $5–50 per deep run and put it in your engagement cost model.
  • Legal: an autonomous agent that decides its own actions is more dangerous under a narrow scope, not less. Run it only against explicitly authorised targets, inside your scope guard, never with "explore the network" freedom.
Answer to your question in one line: yes — start with Kali + Caido + ZAP + Nuclei + Prowler + MobSF + garak + Strix, all free, and buy Burp Pro out of your third client's payment.

12 · Law, licences, contracts — and what NOT to do

Do you need a licence or certificate? No.

Security testing for private companies in India is not a licensed profession. There is no registration, no bar council, no mandatory certification. You need a business registration and GST like any service business, and that's it. The only gate that exists anywhere in the chain is CERT-In empanelment, and that binds the buyer's obligations, not your right to trade (section 04).

The law that actually governs you

ProvisionWhat it saysWhat it means for you
IT Act s.43Civil liability for accessing, downloading, damaging or disrupting a computer resource without authorisation. Compensation up to ₹1 crore.An unauthorised scan is a civil wrong even if you break nothing.
IT Act s.66Criminal: dishonest/fraudulent s.43 acts — up to 3 years imprisonment and/or ₹5 lakh fine. Unsolicited penetration testing falls here.The line between "security researcher" and "accused" is one signed page.
IT Act s.66C / 66DIdentity theft / cheating by personationGoverns phishing simulations and social engineering. Never impersonate a bank, a government body, or a real third-party brand.
IT Act s.72 / 72ABreach of confidentiality and disclosure of information in breach of contractYou will hold client data. Leaking it is criminal, not just a contract breach.
DPDP Act 2023 + Rules 2025You become a data processor the moment you touch client personal dataYou need a written data processing agreement, purpose limitation, deletion timelines, and breach reporting. Penalties to ₹250 crore sit on the fiduciary — and they will push liability to you by contract.
CERT-In directions (6h reporting)Incident reporting within 6 hours; log retention obligationsApplies to your own firm too. Know it before a client asks.
The entire legal question reduces to one sentence: consent makes it legal, and the contract must specify the exact extent of the scan. Scanning with permission is lawful; scanning without it is an offence. Everything below is just how to prove consent existed.

Paperwork you must have before touching anything

  1. MSA (Master Services Agreement) — the commercial frame: payment terms, liability cap, IP, confidentiality, term.
  2. SOW / Scope document — the exact domains, IPs, app URLs, API endpoints, app package names, cloud account IDs. Anything not listed does not exist.
  3. Rules of Engagement (RoE) — test window (dates + hours), permitted techniques, explicitly excluded techniques (DoS, social engineering, physical), rate limits, emergency stop contact and phone number, out-of-band critical-finding notification within X hours.
  4. Written authorisation letter — signed by someone who can bind the company and owns the assets (CTO/CEO/CISO, not a developer). Include: asset list, dates, your name/company, tester identities, source IPs, and an indemnity for authorised acts. Keep it for 7 years.
  5. Third-party permission where the asset isn't the client's: shared hosting, a SaaS the client only rents, a payment gateway, a partner API. Their contract with you is not permission to test someone else's box.
  6. Cloud provider terms — AWS permits customer testing on a defined list (EC2, RDS, API Gateway, Lambda, S3 among them) and requires a Simulated Events form for covert adversarial simulations or C2 hosting. DoS/DDoS simulation is prohibited on AWS and on Azure under all circumstances.
  7. NDA (mutual) and Data Processing Agreement under DPDP.
  8. Insurance certificate — PI cover; increasingly asked for by mid-size clients.

What NOT to do — the list that keeps you out of court

  1. Never test without a signed authorisation naming the exact assets and dates. Not "they said yes on a call." Not a WhatsApp thumbs-up. Signed.
  2. Never accept authorisation from someone who doesn't own the asset — a dev, a project manager, a vendor. Ask "who can sign for this domain?"
  3. Never run DoS, DDoS, stress or load tests. AWS and Azure prohibit it absolutely. Put it in your RoE as excluded, in writing.
  4. Never scan an asset to "demonstrate value" before you're hired. Passive OSINT from public sources is fine; an active scan is a s.66 offence. This is the single most common way a well-meaning founder gets a legal notice.
  5. Never touch Indian government, UIDAI, or bank assets in any form of unsolicited testing. There is no "responsible disclosure" defence worth the risk here.
  6. Never exfiltrate real personal data. Prove access with a count, a masked sample, or a screenshot with redaction — never a database dump. Under DPDP that dump makes you the breach.
  7. Never keep client data after the retention window. Delete evidence on schedule and send a written deletion confirmation. Make it a selling point.
  8. Never name a client or publish a finding without written consent — not on LinkedIn, not in a case study, not as "a leading fintech." Ask, get it in email, then publish.
  9. Never run intrusive checks on production without a signed window and confirmation the client has a recent backup.
  10. Never use cracked or pirated tools.
  11. Never social-engineer employees without explicit written sign-off from HR and legal, and never impersonate a government body, bank or real brand.
  12. Never sit on a critical finding until report day. Out-of-band notification within hours — it is both ethical and the best upsell you will ever make.
  13. Never accept unlimited liability. Cap it at the fee value, exclude consequential loss, carry PI insurance.
  14. Never claim empanelment, certification, or a client you don't have.
  15. Never let an autonomous AI agent roam. Scope guard on, target list explicit, audit log running.

13 · Trust when nobody knows you

This is your real problem, not competition. A stranger is asking a company for credentials to their production system. Here is how unknown testers become trusted ones — in order of impact per hour spent.

1. Publish a redacted sample report

The highest-converting asset you will ever make. Take a deliberately vulnerable app (DVWA, Juice Shop, a lab target), test it properly, and produce the real deliverable — exec summary, findings, evidence, remediation, compliance mapping. Link it on your homepage. Buyers cannot judge your skill; they can judge your report, and they will.

2. Fixed prices, published

Almost every Indian competitor hides pricing. Publishing "Web app audit — ₹55,000, 5 working days, retest included" removes the fear of being fleeced and pre-qualifies every lead who contacts you.

3. Findings write-ups, twice a week

Anonymised, technique-focused: "how an IDOR in a coupon endpoint let any user apply another user's discount." Not thought-leadership. Actual findings. This is what makes a CTO think this person actually tests things.

4. A CVE or a public disclosure

One acknowledged CVE, or a credited disclosure to a known product, outranks three certifications in a technical buyer's mind. Do it through proper vendor disclosure channels — never through unsolicited testing of an Indian company.

5. The paid mini-audit

₹10,000–15,000, 1 day, external surface + cloud config. Low enough to approve without a committee, real enough to prove quality, and it converts to a full engagement roughly a third of the time. This is your single best trust mechanism. Never do it free — free work is valued at what it costs.

6. Insurance + contract as a sales asset

Lead with it: "We carry ₹25 lakh professional indemnity, we sign a DPDP data processing agreement, and all evidence is deleted in 90 days with written confirmation." Freelancers can't say this. It is how you charge 3× a Fiverr tester.

7. Show up at null / OWASP chapters

India's security community meets monthly in Bangalore, Pune, Delhi, Hyderabad and more. Give one 20-minute talk. Every serious Indian security consultancy was built on these rooms, and a talk buys more trust than a year of cold email.

8. Borrow trust from a partner

A compliance consultant, dev agency or MSP introducing you transfers their credibility to you instantly. In year one, borrowed trust closes more deals than earned trust. See the next section.

17 · What to give, what never to give

Give generously

  • Full findings with real reproduction steps
  • Remediation with actual code/config snippets
  • One free retest within 30 days, in writing
  • A 30-minute walkthrough call with their developers
  • The one-page attestation letter they can forward to buyers
  • Compliance mapping (ISO / SOC 2 / PCI / DPDP)
  • Immediate out-of-band alert on any critical finding
  • Written confirmation that evidence was deleted
  • Honest "we found nothing critical" when that's true

Never give

  • Raw scanner output as a report (that's a ₹95/month product)
  • Your scripts, templates, or platform access
  • Unlimited retests or open-ended support
  • Fixing the code yourself for free — that's a separate paid engagement, and fixing what you audited compromises your independence
  • Scope creep: "can you also check this other app?" = new SOW
  • Work before the advance clears
  • A discount for "we'll give you more work later"
  • Testing outside the signed scope, ever, even as a favour
  • Guarantees that they are "secure" or "unhackable" — you certify a point-in-time assessment, nothing more
  • Free work to prove yourself — charge ₹12,000 instead

18 · Should you build the testing suite as a product?

Selling your automation as a SaaS 5.5 / 10

Against it: Astra ($1,999–5,999/yr per target, AI-augmented but human-led), Intruder ($299–499/mo, AI web app pentests from $3,500), Pentest-Tools ($95–190/mo), plus Cobalt, HackerOne, Detectify and a dozen funded PTaaS platforms. They have years of engineering, security researchers, integrations, and marketing budgets. A solo-built scanner competes on nothing.

For it, narrowly: none of them is priced or positioned for the Indian SMB at ₹5,000–8,000/month with local support, INR billing, GST invoicing and compliance mapping to Indian frameworks. That is a real gap — but it is a gap worth entering only after your services business is producing ₹2L+/month and has taught you exactly which 6 features clients ask for.

Verdict: build the platform as your internal delivery engine (9.0/10). Sell it as a product only in year 2, and even then sell it as "managed continuous testing" — software plus a human — not as raw software. The human is the moat; the software is the margin.

20 · How this business dies — the five failure modes

  1. You build the platform for nine months and never sell anything. By far the most likely death for a technical founder. Antidote: first ten engagements delivered with scripts and a template, no exceptions.
  2. You take a job without proper authorisation and something goes wrong. One s.43/s.66 notice and you are finished, insured or not. Antidote: signed scope + RoE + authorisation, every single time, no favours for friends.
  3. You break production during a test. Antidote: RoE with excluded techniques, approved windows, rate limits, confirmed backups, a stop-phone-number, and read-only-first sequencing.
  4. You compete on price and land at ₹25,000 per app forever. Antidote: never be the cheapest; be the fastest and the clearest. Publish prices and hold them.
  5. You stay a project shop and restart at zero every month. Antidote: every project engagement ends with a retainer offer, made on the walkthrough call while they still feel the risk.

Yes — ₹6,000–12,000 realistically. The whole tool stack is free; you're paying for a VPS, a domain and email.
Do I need Burp Suite?
Not to start. Caido Community + ZAP + Nuclei cover you. Buy Burp Pro (~₹40k/yr) from your third client's payment, mainly for extensions like Autorize and for the trust signal.
Can I just use Strix or another open-source AI agent?
Use it — for the fast breadth pass. Never as the deliverable. It costs LLM tokens ($5–50 per deep run), it hallucinates, and it misses business logic entirely. Human-validate everything before it enters a report.
Can the whole engagement be automatic — link, creds, done?
About 55–65% of a web app engagement, and up to 80% of a cloud review. The remaining part — business logic, IDOR across roles, chaining, impact judgement — is the part clients are paying for. Automation is your margin, not your product.
Is there real money in this?
Yes. India cybersecurity $6.56B in 2026 → $15.06B by 2031 at 18.07% CAGR, PTaaS growing 22.6% CAGR globally, and India short 800k–1M professionals. ₹2–3 lakh/month is a realistic month 7–12 target for one person.
Is there competition?
Yes, substantial. Hundreds of Indian audit shops, funded global PTaaS platforms, thousands of freelancers. The soft spots are speed, price transparency, included retests, LLM/AI testing, and vertical depth.
Should I do hardware testing?
Not in year 1. Tiny Indian buyer pool, ₹1.5–4 lakh lab cost, needs physical space, cannot be automated, devices must be shipped to you, and the compliance money routes through accredited labs (TEC/MTCTE/IC3S), not consultants. Sell "IoT ecosystem testing" — app + cloud + update mechanism — instead.
Should I offer general QA testing too?
No. Different buyer, worse margins, and it destroys your positioning as a specialist.
Is LLM red teaming actually a market or hype?
Both. Small buyer pool today, rising fast, almost nobody serving Indian mid-market, prompt injection is #1 on OWASP LLM Top 10 for the second year. Attach it as a ₹40k add-on now; make it a standalone line as demand grows.
What if a client asks whether I'm CERT-In empanelled?
Say no, immediately and plainly. Then offer pre-audit remediation testing so their statutory audit passes first time. Never fake it — it's checkable in 30 seconds.
Can I scan a company's website to show them their problems and win the deal?
No. Unsolicited active testing is an offence under IT Act s.66. Passive OSINT from public sources is fine. Show findings from your own lab instead.
What paperwork must exist before I touch anything?
MSA, SOW with exact assets, Rules of Engagement, a signed authorisation letter from someone who can bind the company, mutual NDA, and a DPDP data processing agreement.
Whose signature counts as authorisation?
Someone who owns the asset and can bind the company — CTO, CEO, CISO. Not a developer, not a project manager, not a vendor.
Can I test a client's app hosted on shared hosting or a third-party SaaS?
Only with that provider's permission too. The client's consent covers the client's assets, not somebody else's infrastructure.
Am I allowed to test things on AWS and Azure?
Yes for your client's own resources — AWS permits testing on a defined service list and requires a Simulated Events form for covert simulations or C2 hosting. DoS/DDoS simulation is prohibited outright on both AWS and Azure.
What is my biggest legal risk?
Testing outside scope, and holding client data. Scope guard in your tooling, signed asset lists, masked evidence only, 90-day deletion with written confirmation.
Do I need insurance?
Not legally, but yes practically. ₹25L cover runs ₹8,000–18,000/yr; ₹50L for a freelance IT consultant ₹10,000–30,000/yr. It also doubles as a sales asset that freelancers can't match.
Sole proprietorship or company?
Start as a proprietorship. Move to LLP or Pvt Ltd (₹8–15k) when procurement demands it or when you want liability separation — usually month 4–8.
How do I bill foreign clients?
GST registration + LUT (both free) lets you export services without IGST. Get paid via Wise/Payoneer or a bank SWIFT arrangement; keep FIRC/FIRA records.
How do I get trusted when nobody knows me?
Redacted sample report on your homepage, published fixed prices, twice-weekly findings write-ups, a paid ₹12,000 mini-audit as the entry point, insurance and contracts up front, and one partner who introduces you.
What is the single best client channel?
Compliance consultants doing ISO 27001 and SOC 2. They sell the certificate, can't do the test, and their client is on a deadline. One good partner is worth more than a year of cold email.
How do I handle "we don't want to be tested"?
Start read-only (cloud config, passive external), give them the excluded-techniques list in writing, and offer the ₹12,000 one-day check. Then find their real trigger — an audit, a customer questionnaire, an insurance renewal. No trigger, no deal.
Should I do free work to get started?
No. Charge ₹12,000. Free work is valued at what it costs, and free clients are the most demanding ones you will ever have.
What are my payment terms?
50% before testing, 50% before report release. Never after delivery — the report is your only leverage.
What if I find nothing?
Say so clearly and explain what you tested and how. Then sell the retainer — "nothing today" is a point-in-time result, and they now understand that. Never invent findings to justify a fee; that reputation kills faster than an empty report.
Should I build the SaaS product?
Not first. Internal tool 9.0/10, sold as SaaS 5.5/10 against Astra/Intruder/Pentest-Tools. If you ever sell it, sell "managed continuous testing" — software plus a human.
Is OSCP worth it?
Real credibility, $1,699–2,749 (~₹1.5–2.4 lakh). Buy it from profits in year 2. Cheaper credibility first: BSCP, a published CVE, HTB/THM rank, and your own write-ups.
How many engagements can one person handle?
4–6 per month with automation, if you protect two days a week for sales. Beyond that you need an analyst for triage and report drafting.
When do I hire?
When you've turned down work two months running. First hire is a junior analyst for triage and report drafting (₹35–50k/month) — not another senior tester.
What's the fastest path to my first ₹1 lakh?
Two partner conversations that produce three ₹12,000 mini-audits, one of which converts to a ₹55,000 full engagement, plus one ₹45,000 cloud review. Achievable in 45–60 days if you start outreach in week two.
What would make me abandon this?
If after 90 days of real outreach — 35+ partner conversations, published sample report, weekly content — you have zero paid mini-audits, the problem is positioning or sales, not the market. Fix those before quitting, because the market data says the demand exists.
