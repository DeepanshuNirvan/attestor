## Practitioner findings that changed the design

- **Traditional DAST reports 40–70% false positives.** Purpose-built agentic systems that only report
  what they can reproduce get under 2%, but they miss the business-logic class entirely. This is the
  whole reason every automated finding enters the review queue as `candidate` and no tool output can
  reach a report unconfirmed.
- **Session death mid-scan is the most common cause of a worthless authenticated scan.** Every
  authentication profile therefore carries a *logged-in indicator* and a re-authentication routine,
  and requests issued after a detected logout are re-queued rather than parsed.
- **The access-control matrix is the automatable half of IDOR hunting.** Recording a per-role request
  corpus during authenticated crawl and replaying it cross-role finds real bugs; comparing responses
  needs a similarity heuristic rather than equality, because timestamps and CSRF tokens differ on
  every response.
- **One missing header across 40 endpoints is one finding.** Correlation by root cause before render
  is the single behaviour that separates a professional report from a scanner export.

## Cloud provider testing policy (summarised, stored for in-console acknowledgement)

Verbatim policy text and the URL live in `packages/policy/src/cloud-testing-policies.ts` and are
shown for acknowledgement before any cloud or network run. Summary as at 2026-08-23:

- **AWS** — customer security testing is permitted without prior approval against a defined list of
  services. Simulated events (covert red team, C2, phishing simulation) require the Simulated Events
  form. **DoS, DDoS, simulated DoS and simulated DDoS are prohibited outright.**
  <https://aws.amazon.com/security/penetration-testing/>
- **Azure** — customer testing of own resources is permitted under the unified penetration testing
  rules of engagement. **DoS testing of any kind is prohibited.** Azure runs a separate DDoS
  simulation programme through approved partners only.
  <https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/msrc/pentest-rules-of-engagement>
- **GCP** — no prior notification required to test your own projects; testing must stay within the
  Acceptable Use Policy and must not disrupt Google's infrastructure or other tenants.
  <https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/6262505>

The platform contains no DoS capability at all, so the prohibited class is unreachable rather than
merely disallowed.

## Business research carried over from `security-audit-agency-2026.html`

- Indian entry price for a basic web app pentest is already ₹25–30k against hundreds of shops. The
  defensible wedge is turnaround, evidence quality, and the newer surfaces (LLM/AI, cloud posture,
  supply chain) — not price.
- **CERT-In empanelment is an organisation-level status.** It gates banks, NBFCs, payment aggregators
  and gateways, SEBI-regulated entities, insurers, government and PSUs, NIC-hosted systems,
  UIDAI-linked systems and critical infrastructure. It does **not** gate SaaS and product startups,
  D2C, ecommerce, edtech, the non-regulated parts of healthtech, ISO 27001 or SOC 2 work,
  questionnaire-driven enterprise sales, or **any foreign client**.
- Forcing functions that actually make someone buy: DPDP Act 2023 with rules notified 13 Nov 2025
  (full compliance 13 May 2027, penalties to ₹250 crore), ISO 27001:2022 control A.8.8, SOC 2 Type 2
  for Indian SaaS selling into the US, PCI DSS 4.0 requirement 11.4, enterprise vendor security
  questionnaires, cyber-insurance underwriting, and app-store data-safety declarations.
- Payment terms that keep small Indian service firms solvent: 50% advance before testing starts, 50%
  before the report is released. The lifecycle state machine models both gates because "the report is
  the leverage; once it is delivered you have none".
