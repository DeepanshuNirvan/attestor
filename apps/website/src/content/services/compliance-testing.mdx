Nobody buys a security test because they feel unsafe. They buy because a date, a contract or a deal
forces them. This service exists for that case, and the work is the same work — what changes is that
the output is shaped so the auditor accepts it without a second round.

## What each framework actually wants

**ISO 27001:2022** control A.8.8 requires that technical vulnerabilities are identified, evaluated
and remediated, with dates. An auditor wants to see the finding, the decision, the fix and the
verification. That is exactly the assessment-then-retest cycle, so the retest report is not a nicety
here — it is the evidence.

**SOC 2** CC7.1 wants evidence of vulnerability identification, and CC6.1 wants evidence that logical
access controls restrict access to authorised users. The access control matrix section of our report
is written to be handed over for CC6.1 directly.

**PCI DSS 4.0** requirement 11.4 wants an annual external and internal penetration test and 11.4.4
wants evidence that exploitable findings were corrected and testing repeated. Note the last part:
without a retest report, 11.4.4 is not satisfied.

**India's DPDP Act 2023** requires reasonable security safeguards. There is no certificate for this
and there is no prescribed test, which means what you need is defensible evidence that you looked,
found things and fixed them. The rules were notified in November 2025 and the compliance date is
May 2027; the organisations that are comfortable in 2027 are the ones building the evidence trail
now.

## What we do not do

We do not certify you. We are not an accreditation body, we are not empanelled by CERT-In, and a
report that claimed to certify compliance would be worth nothing to your auditor and would
embarrass you in the audit. The mapping appendix in your report says which findings touch which
control, and it says plainly that it is a mapping for your convenience and not a compliance opinion.

## Working with your compliance consultant

If you already have an ISO or SOC 2 consultant, introduce us. They sell the certificate and manage
the programme; they usually cannot do the technical test, and the auditor demands one. That split is
the cleanest working relationship in this business and we are happy to be the technical half of it —
including talking directly to your auditor about methodology, which saves you being the translation
layer.

## For regulated entities

If you are regulated by the RBI, SEBI or IRDAI, your statutory audit needs a CERT-In empanelled
organisation and we are not one. We will tell you that on the first call rather than after you have
paid. What we can do is the pre-audit testing and the remediation verification so that audit passes
first time, which is usually cheaper than failing it once.
