We test the application the way somebody with an account would attack it, not the way a scanner
crawls it.

That distinction is the whole engagement. An unauthenticated scan finds missing headers and an
out-of-date library. It does not find that your support role can read an enterprise customer's
invoices, that the checkout accepts the total from the browser, or that a coupon can be applied
twice if two requests arrive together. Those are the findings that change what your buyer thinks of
you, and every one of them requires somebody logged in as two different users at once, comparing
what each can reach.

## How the five days are spent

Day zero is machine time. Discovery, crawling, the tool suite, evidence capture — all of it runs
unattended inside your agreed window and rate limits, and none of it produces a finding you will
ever see. It produces a queue of candidates.

Day one is triage. Every candidate gets reproduced by hand or discarded. Tools in this category
report between forty and seventy per cent false positives; passing that on to you would be charging
you to read noise.

Days one to four are the work. Access control across every role pair, both directions — one role
against another, and one customer against another customer in the same role. Business logic:
workflow order, price and quantity handling, race conditions on anything limited. Multi-step flows,
password reset, session handling. And then the part that separates a five-day engagement from a
one-day scan: working out what several small findings become when chained, and writing that up as a
narrative with a real business outcome at the end of it.

Day five is the report and a thirty-minute call with the people who will do the fixing.

## What we need from you

Two accounts per role. Not one — two. Half of access control testing is checking whether customer A
can reach customer B's data, and that is impossible with a single account per role. If your
application has customers, admins and support, that is six test accounts. They can be disposable and
they should be, because we will tell you to rotate them when we finish.

A staging environment that matches production is better than production. If it has to be production,
say so early: we halve every rate limit, suppress state-changing requests on the first pass, and
agree a stop-the-test contact who is reachable throughout.

## When to buy the complex tier instead

Four or more roles, multiple tenants, payment or approval workflows, or an application big enough
that five days would be a fiction. The complex tier is eight days and adds tenant isolation testing
across separate customer organisations, settlement and approval workflow abuse, and a longer chained
exploitation phase. If we think you need it we will say so on the scoping call, and if we think you
do not, we will say that too.
