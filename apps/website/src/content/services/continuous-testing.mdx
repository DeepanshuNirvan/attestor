An annual penetration test tells you what was true on one day, about a version of your product you
have since replaced eleven times. That was a reasonable model when releases were quarterly. It is
not one now.

The retainer replaces it with something that matches how you actually ship.

## What happens each month

Scheduled rescans inside your agreed windows, with the results diffed against the previous run. Three
things come out of that diff and they are not the same thing:

**New findings** — something you shipped introduced an issue.

**Resolved findings** — evidence for your auditor that remediation happened, with dates.

**Regressions** — a finding that was fixed and has come back. We flag these loudly and separately,
because a regression is not a security problem, it is a release process problem, and fixing the
finding again without fixing the process means seeing it a third time.

Alongside that, attack-surface monitoring: new subdomains, new open ports, new services, certificates
approaching expiry, and credentials appearing in public repositories. Most of what this catches is a
Friday-evening deploy that put something internal on the internet.

Every quarter, a full deep assessment — the same engagement as a standalone audit, with a report and
an attestation letter you can send to a buyer.

## What you get that is not testing

A named contact for the security questionnaire that arrives with every enterprise deal, with answers
already written and kept current in your portal. An out-of-band call when something critical turns
up, not an email three weeks later. And a person who knows your architecture well enough that the
answer to "is this design safe" takes ten minutes rather than a discovery process.

## Fractional security lead

The larger tier adds the things a company needs when the questionnaires stop being occasional:
security questionnaire and due-diligence support, policy and control design for an ISO 27001 or SOC 2
programme, architecture and design review on new features before they are built, and a direct line to
your engineering team.

It is deliberately not a full-time hire. Most companies at this stage need somebody for five days a
month and a name their buyers can be given, not a salary.

## Honest limits

A retainer is not a substitute for fixing things. If findings sit open for six months the monthly
report becomes a list of the same items with older dates, and we will say so. We put SLA timers on
open critical and high findings and escalate them, because a retainer where nothing gets fixed is
just an expensive subscription to bad news.
