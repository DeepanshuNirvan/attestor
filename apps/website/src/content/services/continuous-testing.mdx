An annual penetration test tells you what was true on one day, about a version of your product you
have since replaced many times. That worked when releases were quarterly. It fits less well if you
ship weekly.

A retainer tests on the same schedule you release on.

## What happens each month

We rescan inside your agreed windows and compare the results against the previous run. Three
different things come out of that comparison:

- **New findings.** Something you shipped introduced an issue.
- **Resolved findings.** Evidence for your auditor that remediation happened, with dates.
- **Regressions.** A finding that was fixed and has come back.

We report regressions separately and prominently. A regression usually points at the release
process rather than the code, and fixing the finding again without fixing the process means you will
see it a third time.

We also monitor your attack surface: new subdomains, new open ports, new services, certificates
close to expiry, and credentials appearing in public repositories. Most of what this catches is a
Friday deploy that put something internal on the internet.

Every quarter you get a full assessment, the same as a standalone audit, with a report and an
attestation letter you can send to a customer.

## What you get besides testing

A named contact for the security questionnaires that arrive with enterprise deals, with answers
already written and kept up to date in your portal.

A phone call when something critical turns up, rather than an email three weeks later.

Someone who knows your architecture well enough to answer "is this design safe" in ten minutes.

## Fractional security lead

The larger tier adds what a company needs once questionnaires stop being occasional: questionnaire
and due-diligence support, policy and control design for an ISO 27001 or SOC 2 programme,
architecture review on new features before they are built, and a direct line to your engineering
team.

This is deliberately not a full-time hire. Most companies at this stage need about five days a month
and a name they can give their customers.

## Limits

A retainer does not replace fixing things. If findings stay open for six months, the monthly report
becomes the same list with older dates, and we will tell you so.

We put SLA timers on open critical and high findings and escalate when they expire.
